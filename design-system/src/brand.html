What we are fixing

Both source sites are SharePoint-era builds: navigation organised around the ministry org chart rather than the citizen task, decorative hero carousels above the fold, three separate icon fonts loaded at once, and text set in greys that sit near the contrast floor. Nothing below is styling for its own sake — each decision answers a specific failure.

Principles

Five rules. When a design decision is contested, these settle it — in order.

01

Say the thing plainly

A requester needs to know what to do, what it costs, and what happens next. Decoration that delays that answer is a defect. No stock photography of handshakes, no carousels, no animated counters.

02

One task per screen

A requisition is long. Break it into steps a person can finish in one sitting, save the draft between them, and never ask twice for something the ministry already holds on record.

03

Bilingual by construction

Arabic is not a translation layer applied at the end. Every component uses logical properties, so dir=rtl mirrors the whole interface with no per-component overrides — and the Arabic face is optically sized to match Latin.

04

Accessibility is the floor

WCAG 2.1 AA is the minimum a public service may ship, not an enhancement. Contrast is machine-verified in CI. Every control is operable by keyboard. Status never depends on colour alone.

05

Everything is traceable

Every request carries a reference number, a current state, a named owner, and a visible history. A requester should never have to telephone someone to find out where a submission sits.

In tension?

Lower number wins

If clarity and beauty disagree, clarity wins. If accessibility and brand expression disagree, accessibility wins. This ordering is the point of having principles rather than preferences.

Lockup

The official emblem of the State of Kuwait. It gets no container, no recolouring and no cropping — width follows from height via --emblem-ratio (774 / 875), and a hairline rule separates it from the wordmark in the standard government arrangement.

On deep surface
emblem Medical Requisitions Ministry of Health · State of Kuwait

One emblem, every surface. There is no reversed, mono or otherwise recoloured variant, and none should be made — the official mark is the official mark, and it sits on the deep navy exactly as it sits on white.

Clear space of at least 25% of the emblem height on all four sides. Minimum size 32px tall — below that the dhow rigging is illegible, so use the wordmark alone. Never recolour, stretch, crop, rotate or add effects; and never place the emblem on a busy photograph. It is an official state symbol representing the State of Kuwait: it may carry the identity of an official government service — the header lockup and this service’s browser icon both qualify — but it may never be used decoratively, as a spinner, watermark or ornament, or on anything that is not an official government service.

Heritage gold

The emblem brings gold into the brand world, so it is tokenised rather than left for someone to eyedrop inconsistently. It is ceremonial only: mastheads, seals, certificates, print furniture. At 3.2:1 on white it fails AA for body text, and it carries no state meaning — it is never a button, never a status, never small text on a light surface.
