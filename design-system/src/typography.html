The pairing

IBM Plex Sans and IBM Plex Sans Arabic were drawn by one team on shared proportions, so an English page and an Arabic page hold the same rhythm and the same colour on the page. That is the whole reason for the change: the legacy pairing of Open Sans with Cairo gives two different textures depending on which language a citizen reads. IBM Plex Mono carries reference numbers and quantities. All three are SIL Open Font License — free to embed, self-host and redistribute, with no per-seat licensing to negotiate.

Arabic runs 8% larger with 12% more leading. Arabic is optically smaller than Latin at the same point size and its ascenders and descenders need more room. The system corrects for this once, globally, with --font-scale-ar and --line-height-scale-ar — never by hand-tuning individual components.

Scale

A 1.200 minor third, rounded to whole pixels so text renders crisply at every step. Body is 16px and never smaller for public-facing prose — 13px body copy is the single most common accessibility failure on both legacy sites.

Numerals

Quantities, reference numbers and currency must not shift width as digits change — a column of figures that jitters is a column nobody can scan. Every numeric cell in the system opts into tabular lining figures.

Rules

Kuwait uses Western digits in both languages, so Arabic pages keep the same numerals — do not switch to Arabic-Indic forms. Currency is KWD to three decimal places, the fils. Reference numbers set in Plex Mono and always rendered dir=ltr even inside Arabic text, so the sequence is not reordered by the bidirectional algorithm.

Bilingual rules

Do
  • Set line length with ch units so both scripts get a comfortable measure.
  • Isolate reference numbers and Latin product names inside Arabic with dir=ltr.
  • Mirror arrows, chevrons and steppers. Mark them .icon--directional.
  • Translate before layout. Arabic copy commonly runs 20–25% longer.
Do not
  • Letter-space Arabic. It breaks the joins between letters and renders the word unreadable.
  • Apply text-transform: uppercase. Arabic has no case; the rule silently does nothing or mangles mixed strings.
  • Fake bold with synthetic weights — load the real 600.
  • Mirror clocks, logos, checkmarks or media playback controls.
